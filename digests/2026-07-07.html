Governance

  • Referendum 1910 has been enacted, introducing major changes to Polkadot staking: nominators are no longer slashable, and the nominator unbonding period is reduced from 28 days to approximately 2 days (2 eras). These changes follow the earlier introduction of a minimum self-stake requirement for validators (via Polkadot Forum Article - Gr33nHatt3R, X Posts - paritytech, X Posts - Polkadot).
  • Referendum 1910 and the Dynamic Allocation Pool (DAP) model have drawn significant critique. A detailed forum post argues that removing nominator slashing reduces Polkadot’s economic security by over 99%, lowering the minimum slashable collateral from ~224M DOT to ~2M DOT. The post disputes the DAP’s core premise and highlights a structural conflict of interest, noting that the largest beneficiary of the change is also a major proposer and voter (via Polkadot Forum Article - sorpaas).
  • Referendum 1913 (Continued Integration & Maintenance of Polkadot Asset Hub) is under scrutiny for not meeting the Web3 Foundation’s published guideline of at least 14 days of public community discussion before submission. A forum post questions why W3F voted AYE despite this (via Polkadot Forum Article - dandan).
  • A new treasury proposal draft is in preparation for SlabVault, a consumer app for provably fair mystery packs of PSA-graded trading cards, aiming for a 10,000 USDT request on the Small Spender track. The proposer seeks advice on OpenGov process and scope (via Polkadot Forum Article - 0xPresc).

Technical

  • Referendum 1910’s staking changes were sequenced after the validator set fully converged on the new minimum self-stake requirements established through Referendum 1909, ensuring the security model remains intact while improving the staking experience for nominators (via Polkadot Forum Article - Gr33nHatt3R, X Posts - Polkadot).
  • Substrate4j, a Java client for Substrate-based chains, is nearing completion. It offers static, type-safe runtime access, pure Java cryptography (including SR25519, Ed25519, ECDSA), a complete SCALE module, and is built on the new Polkadot JSON-RPC spec. The client is published to Maven Central and aims to provide a production-grade, developer-friendly tool for interacting with Polkadot, Kusama, and Asset Hub (via Polkadot Forum Article - mhrsalehi).
  • XCM (Cross-Consensus Messaging) is highlighted as natively integrated into the Polkadot protocol, enabling trustless communication between any chain attached to a Polkadot core, in contrast to bridges that require third-party trust (via Polkadot Forum Article - OpenShore).
