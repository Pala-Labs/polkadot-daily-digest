Polkadot Daily Digest: 2026-09-19

Summary

Web3 Foundation said it wants tighter conditions before supporting the next Fellowship salary proposal, after Referendum 1940’s 4,800,000 DOT request was cancelled by Referendum 1941 at 99.2% aye. Separately, a Kusama position paper proposed negotiating a five-year JAM Prime package with the DOT DAO using US$8.64 million as the reference.

Report

Governance

  • Kusama JAM Prime future: emielsebastiaan published a position paper proposing that the KSM DAO set a negotiation direction with the DOT DAO for “16 total cores for 5 years on JAM Prime,” “450,000 JAMKB-equivalent of footprint,” protected KSM control, and Fellowship-led migration and maintenance, using US$8.64 million from the Kusama Vision Fund as the complete-package reference and settling in DOT. The authors propose a 4 week discussion until 16 October 2026 before revising the proposal to inform a direction-setting Wish for Change. (via Polkadot Forum Article - emielsebastiaan)

  • Fellowship salary funding: In the Polkadot Technical Fellowship OpenDev Call #4 proceedings, Bill Laboon of Web3 Foundation said the Foundation wants two conditions before voting for the next Fellowship salary proposal: people already funded full-time to work on Polkadot should not also be paid by the Fellowship, and the Fellowship should have a clearly specified mandate and responsibilities. The discussion referenced Referendum 1940, which asked for 4,800,000 DOT converted to HOLLAR, and its cancellation by Referendum 1941 at 99.2% aye. (via Polkadot Forum Article - Doordashcon)

Technical

  • JAM parachain service: Bastian Köcher presented the JAM parachain service design in polkadot-sdk#11883, described as a human-readable markdown spec plus a Quint specification. The design generalises the relay-chain pipeline with refine and accumulate stages, introduces locked balances for parachain state footprint, and notes that only 48 KB can pass from refine to accumulate, pushing XCM toward off-chain messaging rather than routing through the chain. (via Polkadot Forum Article - Doordashcon)

  • RFC 175: Sergej Sakač presented RFC 175, which removes the concept of on-demand cores and adds a one-time schedule type to the scheduler for the current on-demand logic to build on. The proceedings state that there has been no progress since introduction, it is waiting for reviews, and no decision was reached on the call. (via Polkadot Forum Article - Doordashcon)

  • Proof-of-personhood stack: George Pisaltu presented the individuality-community work, including people, light people, members and coinage pallets, with some supporting logic shipped in the 2.5 Fellowship runtimes release and the rest expected in the next release. The proceedings note that no decentralised individuality mechanism is enabled yet, so nobody can currently become a person, and describe DIM 2 as the next release. (via Polkadot Forum Article - Doordashcon)

  • Snowbridge circuit breaker: Clara van Staden presented RFC 167, a proactive Snowbridge circuit breaker and counterpart to RFC 166, which was merged on 7 September. RFC 167 would use two leaky-bucket breakers, one on Ethereum and one on Asset Hub, counting gross flows with per-asset limits set by OpenGov and a configurable delay, discussed as 24 hours; no decision was reached on the call. (via Polkadot Forum Article - Doordashcon)

  • Metanode / node services: acatangiu opened an early discussion on consolidating node services for the Polkadot Products Platform, arguing that current separate funding for RPCs, collators and bootnodes is hard to scale. The near-term idea is for validator operators to also run collation for Asset Hub and likely People Chain, with Bulletin Chain opt-in progressively, and with extra hardware requirements compensated. (via Polkadot Forum Article - acatangiu)

Ecosystem

  • Polkadot Devnet app model: Parity said that on the Polkadot Devnet, .dot apps run through the Polkadot App, and the Host API gives apps access to accounts, signing, chain connections, storage and permissions while keys remain with the user. (via X Posts - paritytech)
