Summary

Robonomics 5.0 shipped release 50 “Runtime-First,” moving toward a runtime-only repo and polkadot-omni-node collators, though runtime 50 is not yet enacted. Kusama Shield also published a revised audit proposal after its prior referendum failed late.

Report

Governance

  • Kusama Shield published a revised audit proposal after a prior referendum was NAY-voted on the last day of confirmation; the revised version says retroactive funding was removed, CI was updated, an unused broken Solidity function was patched, and future features were updated. The team describes the audit as a blocker for additional features and as important for projects needing low-cost, privacy-friendly transactions. (via Polkadot Forum Article - RustSyndicate)

Technical

  • Robonomics 5.0 shipped its first step on 15 September as release 50 “Runtime-First”, moving the repo toward a runtime-only model: airalab/robonomics now contains runtime, pallets, chain specs and upgrades, while node/service tooling moved to airalab/robins. Collators run the generic polkadot-omni-node, with the team reporting a workspace change of −21k / +2.5k lines. (via Polkadot Forum Article - ensrationis)
  • Robonomics said runtime 50 is built and published but not yet enacted; live chains remain on 43 for Polkadot and 42 for Kusama, which it describes as deprecated. The release also turns chain specs into a dependency-free crate, removes storage for two retired pallets with single-block migrations, and keeps only MigrateToLatestXcmVersion among the listed migrations. (via Polkadot Forum Article - ensrationis)
  • Robonomics outlined its planned 5.0 resource model for cyber-physical systems, including CPS topology, Scopes, access grants, subscriptions, planned storage on Bulletin Chain, off-chain compute with RISC Zero proofs, and policy rules. It said storage and compute have no code yet, and asked for feedback on production use of polkadot-omni-node and third-party fee payment at the transaction-extension level. (via Polkadot Forum Article - ensrationis)
  • Parity Technologies described Celerity as a way for apps to send signed, short-lived messages between users for real-time interactions such as presence, typing indicators, live status updates, or announcing new content without putting every interaction on-chain. (via X Posts - paritytech)
  • Polkadot said it is working to make privacy the default across its upcoming product stack. (via X Posts - Polkadot)
